# The Nairobi Cold-Chain Market: A Briefing

## Executive summary

Kenya's horticulture exports depend on refrigerated logistics, and Nairobi is the hub that links farms to Jomo Kenyatta International Airport [1]. Post-harvest losses for fresh produce in sub-Saharan Africa remain high, with poor cooling a leading cause [2]. Solar-powered cold rooms have expanded quickly among smallholder aggregators since 2020 [3]. This briefing summarizes market size, the main operators, the policy context and open gaps.

## Market drivers

- **Export horticulture.** Cut flowers, avocados and French beans move by air and need an unbroken cold chain from pack-house to aircraft [1][4].
- **Urban food retail.** Supermarket expansion in Nairobi increases demand for chilled dairy, meat and produce distribution [5].
- **Losses.** Estimates of fruit and vegetable losses after harvest range widely; the FAO reports significant losses between harvest and retail in the region [2].

## Operators and models

Pay-as-you-store solar cold rooms let farmers rent crates of cold storage by the day [3][6]. Large integrated logistics firms operate refrigerated warehousing near the airport [4]. Off-grid refrigeration companies also serve dairy cooperatives [7].

## Policy context

Kenya's national cooling strategy work highlights cold-chain efficiency and refrigerant transitions [8]. Electricity access and cost remain constraints outside the grid [9].

## Gaps

Reliable, recent market-size figures for Nairobi alone are scarce; most published numbers cover Kenya or East Africa as a whole.
